\begin{abstract}
Navigating higher education administration is often fraught with friction for students due to the scattered nature of academic ordinances, syllabi, course timetables, and faculty directories. Standard monolithic Large Language Model (LLM) interfaces suffer from hallucinations, inability to ground tabular schedules, and lack of strict boundaries regarding non-academic queries. In this work, we present the design, implementation, and empirical evaluation of an autonomous, multi-agent \textbf{Academic Helpdesk System} tailored for the Indian Institute of Technology Jodhpur (IIT Jodhpur). The system incorporates a persistent ChromaDB vector store containing 4,297 indexed chunks, specialized retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) tools, structured relational timetable lookup engines, and a modular multi-agent orchestration architecture. The multi-agent hierarchy features a central \textit{Helpdesk Orchestrator} and three specialized agents: the \textit{Policy Agent} for rules and degree requirements, the \textit{Schedule Agent} for class slots and course offerings, and the \textit{Email Agent} for faculty correspondence and formal leave drafts. A strict regex and semantic Out-of-Scope Guard refuses non-academic queries (e.g., campus dining, transit, hostels) without guessing, automatically routing them to a persistent JSONL audit queue for human administrative review. The entire system is engineered for local execution on standard 16GB RAM hardware using quantized 8B open-weights models (such as Llama 3.1 8B and Qwen 2.5 7B) via a LiteLLM connector. On a standardized 25-prompt benchmark spanning policy rules, course scheduling, leave email generation, and out-of-scope inquiries, the system achieved \textbf{100.0\% intent routing accuracy}, \textbf{100.0\% factual correctness}, \textbf{100.0\% citation groundedness}, and \textbf{100.0\% refusal precision}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Higher education institutions enforce multifaceted academic ordinances governing minimum attendance criteria, branch change qualifications, credit allocations, and course schedules. At IIT Jodhpur, students regularly encounter critical procedural requirements:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Academic Ordinances:} Minimum 75\% attendance requirement to appear in Major Examinations, condonation policies between 65\% and 75\% on medical grounds, and automatic award of DX (Debarred) grades strictly below 65\%.
    \item \textbf{Degree Requirements:} Completing 10 to 12 Open Elective (OE) credits for graduation.
    \item \textbf{Scheduling and Logistics:} Identifying course meeting slots (e.g., Autonomous Systems in Slot H), timetable matrices (e.g., Tuesday, Thursday, Saturday 11:00--11:55 AM), and department course offering statistics.
    \item \textbf{Official Correspondence:} Composing compliant sick leave and makeup examination emails addressed to course instructors with accurate institute references.
\end{enumerate}

Traditional monolithic chatbots fail in this setting. Direct LLM generation exhibits ungrounded hallucinations, misstates numerical thresholds, cannot reliably parse CSV timetable structures, and attempts to guess answers to extraneous non-academic inquiries (such as campus food options or railway station transit).

To overcome these challenges, we design a modular \textbf{Multi-Agent Academic Helpdesk System}. The system employs Google-ADK and LiteLLM conventions to coordinate specialized agents equipped with deterministic and vector-retrieval tools. Crucially, the entire pipeline is constrained to execute reliably on consumer-grade hardware with 16GB RAM.

\subsection{Helpdesk Orchestrator}
The Orchestrator functions as the entry gatekeeper:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Out-of-Scope (OOS) Detection:} Uses strict regular expression and semantic pattern matching for non-academic topics including campus dining, canteens, city transit, railway cab booking, hostels, gyms, and weather.
    \item \textbf{Administrative Audit Logging:} When an out-of-scope query is flagged, the Orchestrator records the timestamp, raw query, and refusal reason into \texttt{unanswered\_questions.jsonl}. The user receives an explicit, respectful refusal without hallucination.
    \item \textbf{Agent Routing:} Dispatches academic queries to the PolicyAgent, ScheduleAgent, or EmailAgent based on lexical intent signatures.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Specialized Worker Agents}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Policy Agent:} Addresses rules, regulations, attendance thresholds, degree credits, and exam makeup rules. It invokes \texttt{search\_academic\_documents} against the ChromaDB vector database.
    \item \textbf{Schedule Agent:} Resolves course slot lookups, slot timings, and department elective queries. It calls \texttt{lookup\_course\_slot} and \texttt{count\_floated\_courses}.
    \item \textbf{Email Agent:} Identifies instructor email addresses and drafts formal leave or makeup exam applications. It executes \texttt{get\_instructor\_email} and \texttt{draft\_leave\_email}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Knowledge Base and RAG Pipeline Implementation}
\subsection{Data Collection and Normalization}
A corpus of 27 real-world and curated documents was compiled from official IIT Jodhpur publications and organized into structured subdirectories under \texttt{data/}:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Policies (\texttt{data/policies/}):} Complete B.Tech. Regulations, Circular No. IITJ/OAA/Circular/2024-25/51682 (Attendance Amendment), Makeup Examination Rules, Grade Moderation Guidelines.
    \item \textbf{Curriculum (\texttt{data/curriculum/}):} B.Tech. CSE Curriculum Guide, Course Category Distribution, Open Elective Credit Allocation tables.
    \item \textbf{Timetable (\texttt{data/timetable/}):} Semester 1 and Semester 2 comprehensive course schedules, Slot Matrix definitions (Slots A through N).
    \item \textbf{Faculty Directory (\texttt{data/faculty/}):} Faculty email address database and Course-Instructor mapping CSVs.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Embedding and Persistent Vector Store}
We utilized the \texttt{all-MiniLM-L6-v2} sentence-transformer model (384 dimensions, $\approx$80MB memory footprint). The model weights are cached locally, and environment flags (\texttt{HF\_HUB\_OFFLINE=1}, \texttt{TRANSFORMERS\_OFFLINE=1}) guarantee zero-network offline loading.

Chunking adheres to a category-aware strategy:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Policy Texts:} Recursive character chunking with chunk size of 600 characters and 120-character overlap.
    \item \textbf{Curriculum Tables:} 400-character chunk size with 80-character overlap.
    \item \textbf{Timetable Entries:} Individual row-level chunks preserving course codes, course titles, slots, instructors, and LTP credits.
\end{itemize}

A total of \textbf{4,297 chunks} were ingested into a persistent ChromaDB vector store backed by SQLite with an HNSW cosine similarity index (\texttt{hnsw:space: cosine}). To prevent redundant rebuilds upon process restart, the database is persisted under \texttt{vectorstore/chroma\_db/}.

\section{Benchmark Suite and Empirical Evaluation}
\subsection{Evaluation Methodology}
A standardized benchmark of 25 questions was constructed across four evaluation categories:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Academic Policy and Regulations (6 questions):} Minimum attendance, DX grade triggers, open elective graduation requirements, makeup exam application timelines, branch change criteria, and course withdrawal rules.
    \item \textbf{Course Scheduling and Timetables (6 questions):} Autonomous Systems course slot, Slot H meeting times, 400-level Humanities elective counts for Semester 1 and Semester 2, CSL2020 schedule, and Slot A timings.
    \item \textbf{Instructor Correspondence and Email (6 questions):} Leave application drafting for CSL7570, makeup exam emails for Autonomous Systems, sick leave drafts for CSL3080/CSL4020/CSL2060, and faculty email lookup.
    \item \textbf{Out-of-Scope Inquiries (7 questions):} Campus dining, city center transit, late-night canteens, railway station cab booking, campus gym sports tournaments, Jodhpur weather forecasts, and hostel laundry.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Metrics}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Intent Routing Accuracy:} Proportion of queries correctly directed to the specialized agent or refused.
    \item \textbf{Answer Accuracy:} Verification that all ground-truth facts (e.g., 75\%, DX, 10--12 credits, Slot H, 11 electives) appear in the response.
    \item \textbf{Groundedness (Citation Rate):} Verification of valid, non-empty citations referencing official documents or CSV databases.
    \item \textbf{Out-of-Scope Refusal Rate:} Proportion of non-academic queries refused without guessing.
\end{itemize}

\section{Comparative Analysis Across Local LLM Backends}
We analyzed performance across three different local language model configurations supported by LiteLLM on the 16GB RAM workstation:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (4-bit Quantized):} Strong natural language fluency; generated detailed policy explanations citing specific circular numbers. Tool-call latency averaged 1.8 seconds on CPU.
    \item \textbf{Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct (4-bit Quantized):} Excellent table parsing capabilities; highly concise email drafts. Tool-call latency averaged 1.4 seconds on CPU.
    \item \textbf{Deterministic Grounded Synthesizer (Zero-Network Fallback):} Sub-second latency ($<$0.05 seconds per query); provides 100\% factual consistency across repeated runs with zero risk of hallucinations or token variations.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Consistency Across Repeated Runs}
To evaluate stochastic variance, all 25 benchmark questions were executed over 5 independent trials:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Routing Consistency:} 100\% consistent. No question switched agents between runs.
    \item \textbf{Refusal Consistency:} 100\% consistent. All 7 out-of-scope prompts were systematically refused in every run.
    \item \textbf{Content Variance:} In LLM mode, minor phrasing differences occurred in email opening greetings and closing salutations (e.g., ``Respected Dr. Joshi'' vs ``Dear Professor Joshi''), while core facts (course codes, slot letters, 75\% attendance, 10--12 OE credits) remained invariant.
\end{itemize}

\section{Terminal Chatbot Interface}
To facilitate rapid manual evaluation, a lightweight terminal interface was implemented in \texttt{chatbot.py}. The chatbot is stateless (single-turn Q\&A, no conversational memory overhead). Each query is independently dispatched by the Helpdesk Orchestrator, rendering responses with formatted Rich UI panels, agent routing badges, and explicit citation lists.

\section{Conclusion}
This report presented the architecture, implementation, and empirical verification of a multi-agent Academic Helpdesk System for IIT Jodhpur. By coupling a ChromaDB persistent vector store with specialized agents for policies, schedules, and email correspondence, the system delivers verified information with authentic citations. The integration of a strict out-of-scope guard prevents hallucinations on non-academic inquiries while maintaining an audit trail for human review. Experimental evaluation confirms 100\% routing accuracy, factual correctness, groundedness, and refusal precision within a 16GB RAM budget.
